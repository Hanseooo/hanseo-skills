# Anti-Cargo-Cult Rules

Successful products are examples of possible mechanisms, not recipes.

## Never infer these automatically

- A mascot caused a successful product to win.
- Lifetime pricing is better than subscriptions.
- Local-first automatically differentiates.
- Beautiful UI creates willingness to pay.
- Viral videos create a sustainable business.
- No competitors means opportunity.
- Many competitors means no opportunity.
- AI makes a product more valuable.
- A founder's personal problem represents a market.
- Community upvotes prove payment intent.
- Product Hunt rank proves durable demand.
- A fast build deserves to exist because implementation is cheap.

## Causal transfer test

For any lesson borrowed from another product, answer:

1. What actually happened?
2. What evidence suggests a particular mechanism mattered?
3. What other factors could explain the result?
4. Does the same mechanism exist in the new product?
5. Does the new builder have similar distribution, timing, audience, platform, or domain advantages?
6. What cheap experiment could test the causal assumption?

## Counterexample requirement

When a heuristic starts to look universal, actively look for a counterexample.

Examples:
- beautiful products with weak monetization;
- viral launches with few customers;
- local-first products nobody wanted;
- subscription products with excellent economics;
- mature categories where a new entrant still won;
- empty categories that were empty because nobody cared.

## Copy mechanisms, not aesthetics

Good transfer:
> The output of the product is inherently shareable, creating distribution.

Bad transfer:
> That successful app has a cute mascot, so ours needs one too.
