# Distribution Patterns

Distribution is part of product design.

## Marketplace distribution

App stores, extension stores, plugin ecosystems, templates, and developer marketplaces can provide intent-rich discovery.

Ask:
- Is the target user already browsing here?
- How crowded are search/category results?
- What ranking/review dynamics matter?
- Can platform policy erase the channel?

## High-intent search

Strong when users naturally search the problem at the moment of need.

Good signs:
- stable terminology;
- troubleshooting queries;
- compliance/release/migration moments;
- users already search how-to content.

## Artifact loop

The product's output displays its value to others.

Examples include demos, shareable reports, gifts, designs, or embeds.

## Recipient loop

The user must send the output to another person, exposing the product naturally.

## Creator-demo fit

The core mechanism can be shown visually in a short video without extensive explanation.

Do not confuse demoability with willingness to pay.

## Community distribution

Works best when the builder/user is genuinely part of the community and the product maps to an existing shared behavior.

Avoid extractive promotion.

## Paid acquisition

Valid when:
- value is legible quickly;
- target audience can be reached;
- pricing/LTV supports CAC;
- onboarding converts;
- retention/refund economics are acceptable.

Organic is not morally superior.

## Founder distribution capital

Existing audience, prior products, reputation, communities, partnerships, and media access materially affect launch performance.

Do not generalize another founder's distribution results without accounting for this.

## First-user ladder

For meaningful products, ask:
- Where does user #1 come from?
- What repeats for #10?
- What can plausibly scale to #100?
- What changes at #1,000?

For tiny timing experiments, the ladder may be unnecessary before launch.
