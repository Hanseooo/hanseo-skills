# Pricing Patterns

Choose a model that feels causally connected to value and cost.

## Perpetual / one-time

Good fit when:
- product acts like a tool;
- core processing is local;
- recurring service cost is negligible;
- ownership is part of the appeal.

Possible variant:
`buy current version + keep it forever + updates for a defined period`

## Subscription

Good fit when value genuinely recurs through:
- monitoring;
- collaboration;
- cloud sync/storage;
- ongoing data;
- recurring automation;
- support/service;
- recurring inference/API cost;
- frequent evolving content.

Do not invent recurring billing for a fundamentally one-off artifact unless the broader service justifies it.

## Credits / usage

Good for irregular expensive operations, especially AI/media processing.

## Transaction fee

Good when the product directly facilitates an economic transaction and the fee scales with realized value.

## Freemium

Useful when free usage:
- demonstrates the transformation;
- seeds a network/community;
- creates distribution;
- lowers trust barriers;
- leads naturally to a paid boundary.

## Paid-upfront as positioning

Paid-upfront can itself simplify product psychology and reduce competition in free-download ecosystems, but only when buyers already understand the value.

## Pricing test

Ask:
- What is the user's mental comparison?
- What recurring cost does the product create?
- Does payment timing match the value moment?
- Is the purchase large enough to justify decision friction?
- Would a free tier improve conversion or simply attract non-buyers?
