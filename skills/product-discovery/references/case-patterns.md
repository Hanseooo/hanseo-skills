# Case patterns

Mechanisms observed in independent products, for analogy and counterexample.
Named products are where a pattern was seen, not proof it transfers. Re-check
current facts about any of them before leaning on one.

## Before borrowing a lesson

1. What actually happened?
2. What evidence says this mechanism mattered — and what else explains it?
   Founder audience, prior products, press, timing and platform featuring are
   the usual confounders.
3. Does the mechanism exist in this product, for this audience, platform and
   moment?
4. Does this builder hold the same assets?
5. What cheap experiment would test the transfer?

Transfer: "its output is inherently shareable, so use spreads it." Copy: "it has
a mascot, so ours needs one."

## Patterns

**Mature-category execution** — Tarsi (budget tracking), Screen Studio (screen
recording), Xnapper, LookAway. *Is the category crowded because the job is
valuable, while the existing experience still leaves room for a clearly
preferred version?* Polish alone did not do it in any of these.

**Fix-the-annoyance** — LookAway. *Do people already want the category but drop
existing products over one recurring irritation?*

**Delight and enthusiast affinity** — Keeby. *Is there a community that already
cares about a sensory, expressive or identity experience software can make more
satisfying?* Value with no pain in it at all.

**Personality integrated with function** — Clucky. *Does the personality
reinforce the mechanism, or is it decoration?*

**Intervention point** — one sec. *Does the product win by acting at the exact
moment the behaviour happens, rather than by having more features?*

**Workflow hand-off** — a DAW-to-release licensing check is the shape: one
established product handles one side, another handles the other, and the
transition between them is awkward with no participant motivated to own it. *What
happens immediately before or after a successful product does its job?* The
riskiest assumption there was "will they pay for the hand-off", not "can the
file be parsed".

**Output as distribution** — Screen Studio. *Does normal use create an artifact
that shows the product's value to the next potential user?*

**Recipient loop** — Letter4U. *Does the output reach another person who then
meets the product?*

**Distribution as an operation** — Symmetry; Evelize for paid acquisition.
*Can acquisition become a repeatable production system rather than a hoped-for
viral moment?* Paid acquisition is a legitimate channel when value is legible
fast and economics carry the CAC.

**Outcome-oriented AI** — Josie AI. *Can the user describe the outcome they want
without mentioning AI?*

**Domain or local workflow insight** — Kape Tools. *Does the builder understand
fragmented behaviour, language, paperwork or culture that general products
ignore?*

**Timing launch** — Bendy. The builder already knew the MacBook lid-angle sensor
was readable; a platform keynote supplied the trigger and the analogy; coding
agents made a same-day build possible; strong launch video and existing audience
carried it. Pattern: `preparedness + trigger + analogy + fast build + launch
content + distribution capital`. *Does the upside justify a tightly capped build
over a short commercial half-life?* Remove the preparedness or the distribution
capital and the outcome changes.

**Cross-product leverage** — one founder's public updates across Clucky, Anim8
and Bendy: product A creates a need, product B is built for it, B improves A,
A becomes B's showcase; small fast products fund marketing for the conviction
product. *Does each product have a defined role?* Without role clarity this is
distraction with a theory.

## Counterexamples

Look for one whenever a heuristic starts to feel universal:

- Highly visible launches with very few paid conversions.
- Polished utilities that attracted almost no users.
- Local, private, one-time-purchase apps with no meaningful revenue.
- Crowded AI-era utility categories where many builders shipped the same idea
  within weeks — easy to build means easy to copy.
- Empty categories that were empty because nobody cared.
- Subscription products with excellent economics; mature categories a new
  entrant still won.

Upvotes, comments and downloads measure attention. None of them is payment,
retention or durable demand.
