# Pricing patterns

Pick the model whose shape matches how value is delivered, how often it recurs,
and what ongoing cost it creates — **pricing congruence**. Neither subscription
nor one-time is the default.

| Model | Fits when |
|---|---|
| **One-time / perpetual** | it acts like a tool, processing is local, recurring cost is negligible, ownership is part of the appeal. Variant: buy the version, keep it forever, updates for a set period. |
| **Subscription** | value recurs — monitoring, sync or storage, collaboration, ongoing data, recurring automation, inference cost, evolving content, support |
| **Credits / usage** | operations are irregular and expensive, typically AI or media processing |
| **Transaction fee** | the product facilitates a transaction and the fee scales with value realised |
| **Freemium** | free use demonstrates the transformation, seeds distribution or lowers trust barriers, and leads naturally to a paid boundary |
| **Paid upfront as positioning** | buyers already understand the value; it can simplify the product and stand out among free downloads |

A recurring charge for a fundamentally one-off artifact needs a broader recurring
service behind it.

## Questions

- What is the buyer's mental comparison — what do they price this against?
- What recurring cost does the product create per user?
- Does payment land at the moment value lands?
- Is the price large enough to justify the decision friction it causes?
- Would a free tier convert, or only attract non-buyers?
- For an existing product: what do cohort conversion and refund rates say about
  the current price, before changing it?
